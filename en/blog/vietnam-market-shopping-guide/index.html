Shopping at a Vietnamese market: prices, quantities and useful phrases

A quoted price is only useful when you know what it buys: one item, a bag or a kilogram. Confirm the selling unit alongside the number before placing an order.

Updated 2026-11-18

Look at the traffic before shopping from a motorbike

In some markets observed by EvaKim, customers buy from stalls while seated on their motorbikes. This differs from parking outside a Korean supermarket and walking in. Rules vary between markets. Follow local signs; where lanes are narrow or crowded, park safely and walk.

A different unit changes the price

Ask whether the price is per kilogram, bag or bunch. Having the seller enter the amount into a phone calculator can help you check the number. Ask whether a small quantity is available before the seller packs it. One stall’s price is not a national average.

Confirm the final total

Check the total again if you add items or change quantities. A displayed QR code does not guarantee that your payment app will work. Agree on a payment method with the seller, then check your purchases and change.

Everyday convenience through Eva’s eyes

The useful comparison is not simply which country is cheaper. Someone buying a little at a nearby stall may value different things from someone buying several days of groceries at a supermarket. Observe what shoppers buy and how often they return, as well as the prices.
