Ordering coffee in Vietnam: condensed milk, black coffee and café etiquette

Expected an iced black coffee but tasted sweet condensed milk? Recognising ‘sữa’ and ‘đá’ on a Vietnamese menu makes ordering easier.

Prepared by EvaKim Think Factory

Updated 2026-11-18

At a glanceSữa means milk and đá means ice. Cà phê sữa đá usually contains sweetened condensed milk. To reduce sweetness, ask about condensed milk as well as sugar.

Start with two familiar options

Cà phê sữa đá is iced coffee with condensed milk; cà phê đen đá is iced black coffee. Do not assume black means unsweetened: say ‘không đường’ if you do not want sugar. Recipes and available adjustments vary by café.

Less sweet and less condensed milk are different requests

Condensed milk is already sweet, so removing added sugar may not achieve the taste you want. ‘Ít ngọt’ means less sweet; ‘ít sữa đặc’ asks for less condensed milk. Premixed drinks may be difficult to adjust. Ask first, and request your preferred amount of ice separately.

The seat is part of the experience

Eva and Steve have described café customers sitting side by side looking towards the street. If you expect to sit opposite someone, it can feel unfamiliar. Watching the street together is another way to spend time. This observation does not describe every Vietnamese café.

Ask before bringing food or taking photographs

Permission at one café does not establish a rule for another. Check policies on outside food, photography and seating with the staff. Choose a photo spot that keeps walkways clear and avoids including other customers’ faces.
